# Academic Anti-Patterns

This file adapts the original Humanizer logic to academic writing. Target the patterns below when the text sounds AI-generated or formulaic.

## 1. Inflated Significance

Watch for:
- "pivotal", "transformative", "crucial turning point", "marks a major shift"

Better move:
- State the actual research problem or institutional setting.

## 2. Promotional Adjectives

Watch for:
- "remarkable", "vibrant", "powerful", "groundbreaking", "important and timely"

Better move:
- Replace praise with a concrete description of data, method, or result.

## 3. Vague Attribution

Watch for:
- "studies have shown", "scholars argue", "it is widely believed"

Better move:
- Keep the citation or make the sentence more precise if the source text already names the strand.

## 4. Empty Contribution Claims

Watch for:
- "fills the gap", "enriches the literature", "provides important evidence"

Better move:
- Specify what the paper adds: setting, proxy, mechanism, identification choice, or comparison.

## 5. Generic Policy Or Future Outlook Endings

Watch for:
- "the findings have broad implications", "future research may further explore"

Better move:
- State the bounded implication or omit the line if it adds nothing.

## 6. Stacked Hedging

Watch for:
- "may potentially suggest", "could possibly indicate", "appears to perhaps"

Better move:
- Keep one level of caution that matches the evidence.

## 7. Chatbot Residue

Watch for:
- "I hope this helps", "great question", "let me know"

Better move:
- Delete outright.

## 8. Formulaic Contrast Constructions

Watch for:
- "not only ... but also", "it is not merely ... rather ..."

Better move:
- State the point directly unless the contrast carries real analytical content.

## 9. Transition Clutter

Watch for:
- repeated "moreover", "furthermore", "additionally", "notably"

Better move:
- Keep only the transitions that perform real logical work.

## 10. Triadic Listing For Its Own Sake

Watch for:
- three-item sequences that sound polished but add little precision

Better move:
- Use the number of items the argument actually needs.

## 11. Unsupported Causal Intensifiers

Watch for:
- "proves", "fully demonstrates", "undoubtedly shows"

Better move:
- Use language that matches the design: "is associated with", "suggests", "is consistent with", or "indicates".

## 12. Drift In Technical Content

Watch for:
- variable names rewritten into plain language
- changed sample periods
- altered signs, percentages, or coefficient interpretation

Better move:
- Keep technical content exact. The revision should clean style, not alter substance.
