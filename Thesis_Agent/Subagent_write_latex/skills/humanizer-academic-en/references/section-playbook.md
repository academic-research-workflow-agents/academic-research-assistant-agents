# Section Playbook

Use the subsection that matches the user's current task. Do not load the whole document mentally if one section is enough.
The front-matter additions below supplement the original template rules; they do not replace the base job of the abstract, introduction, conclusion, or keywords section.

## Abstract

Goal:
- State the research question, data or sample, method, main result, and bounded contribution.

Rewrite priorities:
- Remove ceremonial opening lines and generic claims about significance.
- Keep sample period, setting, method, and main findings concrete.
- Preserve cautious wording if the evidence is correlational or the design has limits.
- As a supplemental order, move through question or object -> data and design -> main result -> one necessary mechanism or heterogeneity layer -> bounded implication.
- Allow at most one short scene-setting sentence before landing on the paper's exact task.
- Do not end with ceremonial contribution language.

## Introduction

Goal:
- Define the problem, show the gap, and state the paper's contribution without grandstanding.

Rewrite priorities:
- Replace broad scene-setting with the specific research problem.
- Keep contribution claims narrow and evidence-linked.
- Avoid turning the introduction into a policy speech or a manifesto.
- As a supplemental order, prefer short context -> research question -> literature gap -> design preview -> contribution bundle.
- Keep broad context brief; the empirical question should appear early.
- Method-paper openings can inform discipline and compression, but should not override the thesis introduction's empirical problem-first structure.

## Literature Review

Goal:
- Organize the literature into strands, disagreements, or mechanisms.

Rewrite priorities:
- Prefer contrast and synthesis over name-heavy laundry lists.
- Replace vague phrases such as "many scholars believe" with concrete positioning when the source text supports it.
- Preserve disciplinary nuance and unresolved debates.

## Mechanism And Hypotheses

Goal:
- Explain why the relationship may exist, then state the directional or comparative prediction.

Rewrite priorities:
- Define the mechanism before interpreting it.
- Separate intuition from the formal hypothesis.
- Avoid rhetorical buildup around hypotheses.

## Variables, Data, And Empirical Model

Goal:
- Define variables, describe measurement or construction, identify the sample, and state model design choices clearly.

Rewrite priorities:
- Put the object first: variable, proxy, sample, source, filter, or model.
- Preserve variable names, notation, lag structure, fixed effects, clustering choices, and transformation details.
- Replace abstract prose with operational descriptions.
- Do not simplify away identification logic.

## Regression Interpretation

Goal:
- Report sign, significance, magnitude, and economic meaning accurately.

Rewrite priorities:
- Move in a disciplined order: result, magnitude, interpretation, link to mechanism or hypothesis.
- Keep statements proportional to the evidence.
- Avoid "fully proves", "strongly demonstrates", or similar overclaims.
- Preserve references to tables, columns, and baseline specifications when present.

## Robustness Checks

Goal:
- Explain what changed, what remained stable, and what the robustness exercise implies.

Rewrite priorities:
- Name the substitution or perturbation clearly.
- State whether the sign, magnitude, or significance changes, without overselling.
- Distinguish between "robust", "weaker", and "mixed" results.

## Conclusion And Implications

Goal:
- Summarize the main findings and provide limited implications.

Rewrite priorities:
- Keep policy or managerial implications tied to the actual findings.
- Avoid generic optimism and broad social significance claims.
- End with a bounded takeaway rather than a flourish.
- As a supplemental order, recap the main result first, then the key mechanism or extension, and only then add a bounded implication or boundary statement.
- If the paper already contains mechanism and heterogeneity tests, recall only the most decision-relevant layer rather than repeating every table.
- If limitations are mentioned, use them to clarify boundaries rather than to launch a future-research wish list.

## Keywords

Goal:
- Cover the topic term, object or setting term, and only when needed a method term, using the smallest stable set of keywords.

Rewrite priorities:
- Revise only explicitly provided author keywords; do not invent new ones from the theme.
- English keyword lists usually work best with `3-6` items, often `4`, ordered as topic -> object or setting -> method when method is central.
- Use one stable delimiter, usually commas or semicolons, and avoid sentence fragments or mixed-language baskets.
